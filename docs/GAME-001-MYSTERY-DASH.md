## CORE CARD

### 1. Kid Test
Run around the rec center, investigate suspicious kids, escape the Sneak, and figure out who took the missing item.

### 2. Game Identity / Fantasy
You're hanging out at the neighborhood rec center when the Golden Sneaker trophy goes missing. One of four kids is secretly the Sneak. You've got about two minutes to watch what everyone is doing, find evidence, survive a chase, and decide who actually took it.

The mystery is not "find three things and the game tells you the answer." Everyone can look suspicious. Only one kid fits the full evidence trail.

### 3. Emotional Promise
`Suspicion → tension → "WAIT, I was wrong!" → reconsider → reveal → "run it back"`

The key emotional beat is **changing your mind for a reason**.

### 4. Replay Hook
Every round re-rolls:
- who the Sneak is
- which innocent kid becomes the red herring
- what innocent secret that kid is hiding
- where evidence appears
- which rooms characters visit

Same rec center, different case logic.

The player should want another round because:
> "I thought I knew who it was. Let me try again."

### 5. First 10 Seconds
**0–2 sec:** the rec-center trophy case flashes. The Golden Sneaker disappears with a quick *POOF*.

**2–5 sec:** Nia, Malik, Zuri, and Jayden appear in a fast four-person lineup with `?` bubbles. Text: **ONE OF THEM IS THE SNEAK.**

**5–10 sec:** the chosen detective is already moving in the hallway. One kid glances over their shoulder and hurries around a corner. A nearby visual clue glints. The player can move toward it immediately.

First interaction: `CLUE FOUND +50` with a quick case-board pulse.

No tutorial page. A one-time ghost overlay shows movement + interact controls.

### 6. Core Loop
`OBSERVE → SUSPECT → INVESTIGATE → DISCOVER → RECONSIDER → ACCUSE → REVEAL → REPLAY`

- **Player action:** move room to room, observe character behavior, collect physical/location evidence, investigate suspicious activity, use hiding spots during a chase.
- **Immediate result:** evidence appears on the Case Board; possible matches update without declaring a culprit.
- **Decision:** keep following the current suspect, switch suspicion, investigate another room, or accuse.
- **Reward:** correct accusation → reveal, confetti, detective sticker, score/time bonus.
- **Fail state:** caught by the Sneak → drop one recoverable clue + short funny wobble/stumble. No death. Wrong accusation → record scratch, accusation marked wrong, case continues if time remains.
- **Timer out:** culprit reveal + immediate replay.

### 7. Theme Test
- **Mechanic check:** YES conceptually — observe + chase + overlapping clue elimination + red herring creates a playable reasoning loop. MVP must prove it is actually fun.
- **Identity check:** YES at specification level — named contemporary Black kids, specific faces/hair/clothing/behavior, and a lived-in neighborhood rec center make this something more specific than a generic mystery game with brown avatars.

### 13. Playtest Hypothesis + Kill/Continue
**Hypothesis:** Kids 7–11 who enjoy MM2/Among Us-style suspicion and chase tension will voluntarily replay because the culprit, red herring, innocent secret, and clue placement change — and because new evidence can overturn their first theory.

**Continue if:**
- testers understand the basic objective without a verbal tutorial
- at least some testers voluntarily replay multiple rounds
- we observe a genuine reconsideration moment: "I thought it was ___, but..."
- reveal feels satisfying rather than random
- chase creates excitement without overwhelming the mystery

**Revise if:**
- kids like running but ignore evidence
- kids collect every clue mechanically without forming theories
- red herrings feel unfair instead of funny/suspicious
- character behaviors are too subtle to notice on a phone/Chromebook screen

**Kill / rework the current loop if:**
- accusations remain guesses after repeated rounds
- evidence cannot be understood without adult explanation
- kids do not voluntarily restart
- the chase is doing all the fun work and the deduction layer adds friction

**Future World Connection:** Rec Center. Detective stickers/trophies may eventually transfer to a room/collection in Our World. No world integration in V1.

# DEDUCTION MODULE

## D1. Roles
Four suspects are present every round:

- **1 Sneak** — took/moved the Golden Sneaker.
- **1 Sus / Red Herring** — innocent but hiding an unrelated kid-sized secret.
- **2 Normal / Clearable kids** — may still have overlapping clue traits or suspicious-looking timing, but no major secret.

The four named kids keep their personalities and baseline behaviors. **Roles change; identities do not.**

## D2. Hidden information
At round start the player does not know:
- who is the Sneak
- who is the red herring
- why the red herring is acting weird
- which observed behaviors are relevant to the Golden Sneaker

## D3. Clue logic
Three evidence stages per round.

**Validation rule:**
- Clue 1 must fit at least 3 of 4 suspects OR remain broad enough that it cannot solve the case.
- Clues 1 + 2 must leave at least 2 plausible suspects.
- The complete evidence set must identify exactly 1 valid Sneak.
- At least one later piece should contradict or weaken a plausible early theory.

The game validates the selected clue set before the round begins. If the set does not yield exactly one defensible solution, reroll the set.

### Evidence pool priorities
Prefer a mix of:

**Physical/object:**
- shoe tread/scuff
- clothing thread/fabric
- backpack charm shape
- snack wrapper/napkin mark
- game token
- marker/paint trace
- sports tape
- room-specific object

**Location/time:**
- who entered/leaved a room
- brief visual security snapshot
- object appearing before/after a character visit
- scoreboard/clock/environmental timing cue

**Behavioral:**
- break from normal idle habit
- fleeing a location
- hiding an object
- repeated return to a room
- looking over shoulder

### Hair-as-evidence rule
Hair, braids, beads, locs, or culturally distinctive styling are **not** the default culprit system. Hair accessories may appear occasionally only when a fair story specifically calls for it, but the evidence pool should not teach the player that Black hairstyle markers equal suspicion.

## D4. Red-herring secrets
Initial V1 secret pool:

1. **Broken controller** — kid accidentally cracked a controller and is hiding it because they expect to get in trouble.
2. **Birthday surprise** — kid secretly hid a cupcake/card/small decoration for someone at the rec center.
3. **Mascot/art mistake** — kid accidentally painted or altered part of a rec-center art/mascot project and is trying to fix it before anyone notices.
4. **Snack stash** — kid took/hidden more snacks than they were supposed to have and is trying not to get caught.

Secrets are intentionally small, funny, recoverable kid problems — not adult crimes.

The same secret can be assigned to different named characters only if the behavior/explanation is adapted to that character's personality.

## D5. Character baseline → suspicious deviation
The player gradually learns the cast:

- **Nia baseline:** chats/checks backpack/plays lightly with braid. Suspicious deviation: hurried exit, much faster nervous braid-twisting, avoids a direct path.
- **Malik baseline:** rhythmic basketball dribble. Suspicious deviation: abruptly stops dribbling, tucks ball, avoids eye contact/route.
- **Zuri baseline:** expressive hand gestures/checks art pouch. Suspicious deviation: clamps hands to pouch, quickly leaves art-related area, hides a small object.
- **Jayden baseline:** thumb/controller idle motion. Suspicious deviation: motion stops, hands go into pockets, detours away from game room/hall.

**Sneak behavior:** may also glance back, speed up, reroute, or briefly chase once player pressure rises.

**Important:** being nervous is not proof. Behavior creates a reason to investigate, not an automatic conviction.

## D6. Red-herring fairness
A good red herring has three parts:

1. **Real suspicious behavior** — the player is reasonable to notice it.
2. **Real innocent explanation** — the game did not fake the behavior just to fool them.
3. **Clearing evidence** — the player can discover why that behavior was unrelated to the Golden Sneaker.

Example:

> Malik stops dribbling and bolts toward the game room. The player suspects him. Investigation reveals the controller he broke; separate location evidence places the Sneak near the trophy hall while Malik was dealing with the controller. Malik looked guilty because he *was* hiding something — just not the thing the player thought.

## D7. Case Board
One-button visual board.

Shows:
- Ari/Tre detective icon
- Nia, Malik, Zuri, Jayden portraits
- discovered evidence icons
- known/observed room icons
- `?` unknown state
- `✓` cleared state
- `⚠` evidence overlap / still plausible state

The board displays discovered facts. It does **not** rank suspects or tell the player who is "most likely."

Selecting an evidence icon can pulse the portraits it is consistent with.

## D8. Accusation
Player may accuse from Case Board once at least one evidence item is held. MVP testing should determine whether unrestricted early accusation is fun or whether a two-clue minimum is clearer.

**Correct:** large reveal + score + sticker + remaining-time bonus.

**Wrong:** record scratch + wrong portrait marked; round continues if time remains. Avoid revealing the correct answer immediately unless timer expires.

## D9. Pressure / chase
Target round timer: 120s.

Sneak becomes more reactive when player has gathered meaningful evidence. During a chase:
- music/visual tension rises
- player can use one of five hiding/interact spots
- caught player drops one recoverable clue and gets a short wobble/stumble
- no death, weapon, gore, or elimination animation

The chase should increase pressure, not erase the deduction game.

## D10. Randomization per round
Randomize:
- Sneak
- red-herring kid
- red-herring secret
- evidence placement
- selected clue set
- character routes within safe predefined patterns
- preferred hiding/avoidance area

Do **not** randomize character names, core personalities, canonical appearance, or normal baseline behavior. Familiarity is part of learning to read the cast.

## D11. Deduction success test
A strong playtest should naturally produce a sentence like:

> "I thought it was Malik because he ran, but then I found out why he ran, so now I think it's Nia."

If kids never change their theory for an evidence-based reason, the deduction layer is too shallow.

# Scoring — provisional for build

- +50 evidence discovered
- +100 red-herring secret correctly resolved / suspect cleared
- +200 correct accusation
- +0–100 remaining-time bonus
- −50 wrong accusation, floor 0

Local high score only. Graceful fallback if browser storage is unavailable.

Scoring is secondary to validating **replay + deduction** and may be rebalanced after the first playtest.
